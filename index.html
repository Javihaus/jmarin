What I test

Three kinds of system, one question: can you defend it?

Language models and agents

Customer-facing assistants, RAG pipelines and autonomous agents. When they should hand over to a human, whether answers are supported by the sources, and whether behaviour tested in English holds in Spanish, Catalan or Galician.

Physics-based digital twins

Models of batteries, pumps, plants and grids. Whether the forecast respects the physics, how wide the uncertainty really is, and whether it holds on equipment the model has never seen.

Evidence for governance

Test records a board, an auditor or a regulator can read. Mapped to model-risk practice in banking and insurance, and to the accuracy and robustness duties in the EU AI Act.

Cases

What the tests found

Healthcare

The most accurate triage model was the most dangerous one

Compared three frontier language models on 50 standardised emergency-department cases. Ranked by accuracy, the winner missed 5 of the 28 patients who needed immediate care. The model with lower accuracy missed none of them.

Accuracy 90% vs 86% · missed immediate cases 5 vs 0
Energy storage

Capacity you can commit to, measured in the field

Forecast the remaining capacity of 21 home storage systems over eight years of field records, LFP, NMC and LMO chemistries, each one forecast without seeing its own data. Committing at the lower edge of the 90% band held in 40 of 42 field capacity tests.

40 of 42 tests delivered · typical headroom 11 points
Energy storage

Ordering the battery replacement before it fails

Physics-based digital twin, calibrated on 126 cells and tested on 44 it had never seen. The replacement order came before end of life on every one of them. Presented at the IEEE PES General Meeting 2026.

44 of 44 cells · median 76 cycles ahead · band coverage 96.2% for a 90% band
Banking · credit risk

A credit model that still works three years later

Out-of-time test on mortgage loan data, scoring loans 12, 36 and 60 months after training. A physics-constrained neural network kept its ranking power where gradient boosting lost it. Accepted at IEEE DSA 2025.

AUC at 12 months 0.80 vs 0.61 · at 36 months 0.76 vs 0.62
Energy · contracts

A verifier that never passed a false answer

102 checks on the published supply contracts of a listed energy company and two technical papers, using a logic solver instead of a language model as the judge. No false answer was approved; anything it could not prove went to a person.

0 false answers passed in 102 checks
Multilingual service

English calibration does not travel

Tested whether a hallucination check calibrated in English works in Spanish, Catalan and Galician, on 400 test answers per language. Used as shipped, it did worse than chance. With a multilingual encoder and calibration in each language, it became usable.

AUROC 0.36–0.40 as shipped · 0.74–0.75 calibrated per language
Banking · customer service

An LLM judge that disagrees with itself

Compared four ways of catching invented answers in a retail-banking FAQ assistant. The LLM judge caught the most, but changed its verdict between identical runs. Fixed rules raised no false alarms and left a record an auditor can check.

Judge 11 of 12 correct, not repeatable · rules 0 false alarms
Legal · EU AI Act

The error a grounding score cannot see

Evaluated a compliance agent answering from the AI Act. When it retrieved the wrong article, its answer still looked well grounded. Only a separate relevance check caught it.

Relevance 0.44 vs 0.74 on the right article · grounding score unchanged
LLM software · client

Verification engine for an LLM reliability startup

Formal review of why the original design could not reach the accuracy the product needed, then a redesign with layered checks and escalation to a human on cases no check can decide.

+50% end-to-end accuracy in the team's own evaluation

Marketing Mix Model for a regional McCann agency
